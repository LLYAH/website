What is a stoneware mug?

The short answer

Stoneware is clay fired at a higher temperature than standard ceramic, which vitrifies the body and makes it denser, heavier, and less porous. In hand it feels substantially more solid, and it holds heat noticeably longer, which is the reason people who care about mugs tend to end up here. Love & Love YAH designs each one in-house, makes it to order, and donates 10% of every purchase to a Christian ministry, mission, charity or non-profit.

How stoneware differs

  • Fired hotter — vitrified body, denser and stronger than standard ceramic.
  • Holds heat longer — the thermal mass keeps coffee hot ten to fifteen minutes more.
  • Heavier in hand — usually a third heavier than ceramic at the same volume.
  • Reactive glazes — speckle, drip, and matte finishes ceramic cannot produce.
  • Chip resistant — the dense body survives the drops that chip a thin mug.

Finishes, sizes, and print

Stoneware glazes are the whole point of the material. Speckled and reactive glazes vary slightly from mug to mug, so no two are identical, and that variation is a feature rather than a defect.

  • Sizes — 12oz and 16oz in a straight-sided body.
  • Speckled, matte, and reactive glazes — with natural piece-to-piece variation.
  • Colors — cream, sand, slate, and matte black.
  • Engraved or screen-printed — depending on the glaze.
  • Dishwasher safe — microwave safe unless a reactive glaze notes otherwise.

Stoneware mug questions, answered

What is the difference between stoneware and ceramic?

Firing temperature. Stoneware is fired hotter, which makes it denser, heavier, more chip resistant, and better at holding heat.

Why does my stoneware mug look different from the photo?

Reactive and speckled glazes vary from piece to piece by nature. Every mug is slightly different and that is expected.

Is stoneware microwave safe?

Yes, unless the specific glaze notes otherwise on the product. Reactive glazes with metallic content sometimes are not.

Does stoneware really keep coffee hotter?

Yes, roughly ten to fifteen minutes longer than a standard ceramic mug of the same volume, because of the greater thermal mass.

Is it heavier?

About a third heavier at the same volume. Some people prefer that, some find it too much for a full 16oz.

Can stoneware be ordered in bulk?

Yes, though glaze variation across a large batch is more noticeable than with standard ceramic.
