What is a giclee art print?

Giclee is a printing process, not a paper. It uses a wide-format inkjet with eight to twelve pigment inks instead of the four a standard printer uses, which is what lets it hit the subtle midtones and deep neutrals a four-color press flattens out.

Why giclee for reproducing paintings

  • Eight to twelve ink channels — far more midtone range than four-color printing.
  • Pigment, not dye — ground particles that resist fading for decades.
  • No visible halftone dot — tones blend continuously, like the original.
  • Color-matched to the original — profiled against the painting, not guessed.
  • Prints on rag or canvas — the same process serves both substrates.

Substrates, sizes, and editions

The same giclee file prints differently on rag and on canvas. Rag holds more detail and finer type. Canvas warms the palette slightly and reads as a painting from a distance.

  • Cotton rag paper — 285gsm smooth or 310gsm textured.
  • Canvas — stretched at 1.25in gallery depth.
  • Sizes — 8x10 through 24x36 inches.
  • Open and limited editions — limited pieces numbered in pencil.
  • 1in white border — on all paper prints, for matting.

Care and collecting

Frame paper giclees under UV glazing with an acid-free mat. Keep any print out of unfiltered all-day sun. Giclee print questions, answered

How is giclee different from a regular print?

Giclee uses eight to twelve pigment inks on a wide-format inkjet instead of four dye inks, so midtones and neutrals hold rather than flattening.

Is giclee better than a fine art print?

They are not competing terms. Giclee is the process; fine art print describes the archival materials. Every fine art print here is a giclee.

Paper or canvas giclee?

Paper for detail and type, framed under glass. Canvas for scale and no glare, hung unframed.

Do limited editions come signed?

Limited editions are numbered in pencil on the border. Signing varies by piece and is noted on the product.

Will a giclee fade?

Pigment inks are rated for decades indoors. Direct unfiltered sunlight will shorten that for any print, on any substrate.

Why is giclee more expensive than a poster?

More ink channels, archival substrate, and per-piece color profiling rather than a bulk press run.
