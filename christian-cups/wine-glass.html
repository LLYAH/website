What is an etched wine glass?

An etched wine glass carries the design in the glass rather than on it: sandblasted into the bowl or the base so it cannot wash off, chip away, or fade. On a stemmed glass that matters more than on anything else, because a wine glass is handled by the part that gets scrubbed hardest.

Styles in this collection

  • Stemmed wine glasses — the classic 12oz and 15oz bowls.
  • Stemless wine glasses — more stable, dishwasher friendly, easier to store.
  • Communion and chalice styles — a heavier bowl for a table service.
  • Wedding pairs — two glasses etched with names and a date.
  • Anniversary sets — a year etched into the base.

Sizes, etching, and durability

Etching on a stemmed glass is placed on the lower bowl rather than the rim, so it stays clear of the mouth and out of the dishwasher rack contact points.

  • Stemmed — 12oz and 15oz bowls, 8in tall.
  • Stemless — 15oz, 4.5in tall, far more stable.
  • Sandblast etched — permanent, dishwasher safe.

Etched wine glass questions, answered

Will the etching wash off?

No. Sandblast etching removes surface glass, so it is permanent even through years of dishwashing.

Stemmed or stemless?

Stemless is more stable, easier to store, and better in the dishwasher. Stemmed is the traditional choice for a wedding or a formal table.

Can names and a date be etched?

Yes. That is the most common order here, especially as a wedding or anniversary pair.

Are these dishwasher safe?

Yes, on the top rack. Hand washing extends the life of any thin-walled stemmed glass.

Do you make communion glasses?

Yes. The heavier chalice-style bowl is made for table service and can be etched with a church name.

How should stemmed glasses be dried?

Upright, not resting on the rim, which is the most fragile part of the glass.
